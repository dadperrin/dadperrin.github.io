# Hooking the fuel tank to the helicopter

Helicopter portable fuel tanks were used to deliver diesel fuel to offshore rigs. This helicopter portable fuel tank was a trial of a different design from the usual vertical tanks. I was assigned to  test flying it. I did this on a very cold Saturday morning. I met the helicopter pilot and we flew from the Municipal Airport to the west of Edmonton where the tank was on a flatbed truck parked in someone's field.

The pilot dropped me off and took off again and hovered over the tank. I stood on top of the tank with the big metal loop in my hand to hook it to the helicopter. There is a lot of static electricity generated by rotor blades. I had to be careful and insulated when hooking the tank so I didn't create a ground path. The shock would've thrown me off  to the ground and I likely wouldn't be writing this. The downwash from the main rotor also added to the windchill. I also learned first hand about outwash, caused by the ground effect when the helicopter is close to the ground. It picks up a lot of debris from the ground. I learned to keep my mouth tightly shut.

This version of portable fuel tank was designed to sling starting in a horizontal orientation, which didn't work well. As the helicopter sped up, the tank drag increased and it swung back and up towards the tail rotor.

![](../Files/image-9.png)

This flight behaviour was not popular with the pilots and they had to fly slower than when they were slinging the conventional vertical tank.
