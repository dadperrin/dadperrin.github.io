# Pingo

We put radio towers on top of some. I visited several.

**Pingos** are intrapermafrost ice-cored hills, 3–70 m (10–230 ft) high and 30–1,000 m (98–3,281 ft) in diameter.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingo#cite_note-:52-1) They are typically conical in shape and grow and persist only in [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost "Permafrost") environments, such as the [Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic "Arctic") and [subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic "Subarctic").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingo#cite_note-:62-2) A pingo is a [periglacial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periglaciation "Periglaciation") landform, which is defined as a non-glacial landform or process linked to colder climates.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingo#cite_note-:25-3) It is estimated that there are more than 11,000 pingos on Earth,[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingo#cite_note-4) with the [Tuktoyaktuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuktoyaktuk "Tuktoyaktuk") peninsula area having the greatest concentration at a total of 1,350.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingo#cite_note-:0-5) There is currently limited data on pingos.

[Eddie Gruben](Eddie%20Gruben.md)'s house was on top of one in Tuktoyaktuk.
