The **Bell 204 and 205** are the civilian versions of the [UH-1 Iroquois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-1_Iroquois "UH-1 Iroquois") single-engine military helicopter of the [Huey family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huey_family "Huey family") of helicopters. They are [type-certificated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate "Type certificate") in the [transport category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_category "Transport category") and are used in a wide variety of applications, including [crop dusting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_dusting "Crop dusting"), cargo lifting, Forestry Operations, and [aerial firefighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_firefighting "Aerial firefighting").

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/Bell_XH-40.jpg/220px-Bell_XH-40.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bell_XH-40.jpg)

A Bell XH-40, a prototype of the UH-1 and Bell 204

Bell designed its *Model 204* in response to a 1955 [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army "United States Army") requirement for a utility helicopter. The 204 was a giant step forward in helicopter design, being one of the first to be powered by a [turboshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboshaft "Turboshaft"). The turboshaft engine radically improved the practicality of the helicopter due to its light weight and high [power-to-weight ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-to-weight_ratio "Power-to-weight ratio"), lower fuel consumption, and lower maintenance and operating costs. The use of a turboshaft in the 204 allowed it to carry a useful payload over respectable ranges and at reasonable speeds, which resulted in the 204 and subsequent 205 becoming the most successful western helicopter series in terms of numbers built.[\[1\]](#cite_note-Frawley_Civil-1)

The civil *204B* was first delivered in 1961. The subsequent *Model 205A-1* is equivalent to the *UH-1H*, which, compared to the 204, is longer, larger, and has better performance and a more powerful engine.[\[1\]](#cite_note-Frawley_Civil-1)

Over 60 civil Model 204B helicopters had been delivered by 1967, while further examples were built by Agusta-Bell until 1973. 12,000 Model 205s (including civil 205A-1s) were built by Bell and Agusta-Bell up to the early 1980s. Numerous ex-military 204s and 205s were converted for commercial use.[\[1\]](#cite_note-Frawley_Civil-1)

### Experimental models

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bell_204/205&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Experimental models")\]

* **Agusta-Bell 205BG** – Prototype fitted with two Gnome H 1200 turboshaft engines.[\[6\]](#cite_note-Vectorsite-6)

* **Agusta-Bell 205TA** – Prototype fitted with two Turbomeca Astazous turboshaft engines.[\[6\]](#cite_note-Vectorsite-6)

* **Bell 208** – In 1965, Bell experimented with a single twin-engine Model 208 "Twin Huey" prototype, which was a UH-1D fitted with [Continental XT67-T-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_T51 "Continental T51") twin-pack engine module, consisting of two power turbines driving a common gearbox. This exercise was performed as an experiment using company funds.[\[6\]](#cite_note-Vectorsite-6)

* **205A-1++** – Field-upgraded 205A utilizing a T53-L-17 engine and a 212 drivetrain. Similar to the production 205B and 210.

* **Advanced 205B** – Proposed upgraded Japanese version.\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*\]

* **Global Eagle** – [Pratt & Whitney Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_%26_Whitney_Canada "Pratt & Whitney Canada") name for a modified UH-1H with a new [PT6C-67D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_%26_Whitney_Canada_PT6 "Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6") engine, modified tail rotor, and other minor changes reported to increase range and [fuel efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_efficiency "Fuel efficiency") over the Bell 212.[\[7\]](#cite_note-7)

* **Huey 800** – Upgraded commercial version, fitted with an LHTEC T800 turboshaft engine.\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/HelitackCrew01.JPG/220px-HelitackCrew01.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:HelitackCrew01.JPG)

A Bell 205A-1 with its [helitack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helitack "Helitack") firefighting crew with the [Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Natural_Resources_(Ontario) "Ministry of Natural Resources (Ontario)") in 1995

* **Bell 211** – The *HueyTug*, was a commercial version of the UH-1C with an upgraded transmission, longer main rotor, larger tailboom, strengthened fuselage, stability augmentation system, and a 2,650 shp (1,976 kW) [T55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_T55 "Lycoming T55")-L-7 turboshaft engine.[\[8\]](#cite_note-cent-8)
* **[Bell 212](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_212 "Bell 212")** – Bell Helicopter's company designation for the UH-1N.\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*\]
* **[Bell 214](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_214 "Bell 214") Huey Plus** – Strengthened development of the Bell 205 airframe with a larger engine; optimized for "hot and high" conditions. Later developed into the larger, twin-engine [Bell 214ST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_214ST "Bell 214ST").\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*\]
* **[Bell 412](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_412 "Bell 412")** – Bell 212 with a four-blade semi-rigid rotor system.\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*\]
* **Panha Shabaviz 2-75** – is an Iranian utility helicopter built by the Iranian Helicopter Support and Renewal Company.[\[9\]](#cite_note-Panha-9) It is a [reverse engineered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineering "Reverse engineering") version of the Bell 205s which were sold to the government of [Mohammad Reza Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Reza_Pahlavi "Mohammad Reza Pahlavi"). The first example was built in 1998 and the type was publicly unveiled the following year.[\[9\]](#cite_note-Panha-9) It has been manufactured locally in Iran since 2002 and is in active service with the Iranian military and government. It has also been claimed that it can be modified to carry light weaponry.\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*\]

For all military operators, regardless of the actual model, see *[List of UH-1 Iroquois operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UH-1_Iroquois_operators "List of UH-1 Iroquois operators")*

*Data from* The International Directory of Civil Aircraft [\[1\]](#cite_note-Frawley_Civil-1)

**General characteristics**

* **Crew:** 1 or 2
* **Capacity:** 8-9 passengers / 3,000 lb (1,361 kg) equivalent cargo
* **Length:** 41 ft 8 in (12.70 m)
* **Height:** 14 ft 7 in (4.45 m)
* **Empty weight:** 4,600 lb (2,087 kg)
* **Max takeoff weight:** 9,500 lb (4,309 kg)
* **Powerplant:** 1 × [Lycoming T53-L-11A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycoming_T53-L-11A "Lycoming T53-L-11A") [turboshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboshaft "Turboshaft") engine, 1,100 shp (820 kW)
* **Main rotor diameter:** 48 ft (15 m)
* **Main rotor area:** 1,808 sq ft (168.0 m2)

**Performance**

* **Maximum speed:** 120 kn (140 mph, 220 km/h)

* **Cruise speed:** 111 kn (128 mph, 206 km/h)

* **Range:** 300 nmi (350 mi, 560 km)

* **Service ceiling:** 19,390 ft (5,910 m)

* **Rate of climb:** 1,755 ft/min (8.92 m/s)
